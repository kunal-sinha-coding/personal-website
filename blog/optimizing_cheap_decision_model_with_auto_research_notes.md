# Optimizing a Cheap Decision Model with Auto Research

### TL;DR

I fine-tuned a 1.5B parameter Qwen model to strengthen performance on When2Call, a benchmark evaluating an Agent's ability to decide when to make tool calls. This model achieved 77.45% accuracy, matching or exceeding the performance of decision models at least 18x larger: Cloudflare's Clef (70.96%), OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna with the Decision API (74.06%), and Perplexity's Decider v1.1 27B (76.5%)

I was able to obtain these results more quickly after building an auto-research loop, allowing Codex to automatically explore strategies for optimizing training and inference. The second half of this blog post will dive into the design of the loop, highlighting what worked well and what did not.

## Learnings
- The system is still highly dependent on human guidance in that it's not fully autonomous. Generally, I as a human had to intervene and specify a family of approaches that I wanted to try. But then within the family, the researcher was pretty skilled at exploring a bunch of different possibilities within the family and finding the one that had the best performance. But what you would want optimally is a system that is good at brainstorming the broader family of ideas to come up with. Because at that point, then it would be truly autonomous. Because now what happens is if I have to come up with the family of ideas, then I'm acting as sort of a director or like a senior engineer who is assigning tasks for a junior and very experiment just to run. Or maybe like a. A PI or something for like a project versus if it can come up with families' approaches, right? Then it's acting more like an actual, like, senior person, a director. And at that point, the human barely even needs to be involved. Like at that point, you could just give it a broad goal of hey, like, you know, improve performance on this, and you know, voila, you'll do well. That being said, the existing approach is still pretty easy, right? Like, because most of the workload or the strain of it is taken off of you. You just need to come in with a basic idea, like, hey, why don't we try generating some synthetic data? Or, you know, why don't we try doing RAC? And you just give it a really broad instruction like that, and it comes up with a bunch of experiments in that family. And it writes all the code and does all of them and then gives you just like reports back. So it does make the process a lot simpler.
- Potentially, I'm limited by the model that I used. So I found that. So I, as I said earlier, was using GPT-6 Luna. And it's possible that if I'd used a more powerful model, such as Soul or Hasura, it might have actually been powerful enough to come up with families on its own. Or it may have been. - Another challenge I ran into is that there is a trade-off when it comes to it. I guess it's the standard exploration versus exploitation trade-off that people have talked about in other contexts, like in reinforcement learning. Like, if we're pursuing some family of approaches, how much time and effort should we spend just fully exploring that family versus moving on to another family? And this is a trade-off, right? Because, you know, if you're spending too long inside one family, then it's like you're wasting time. But conversely, if you leave too quickly, then you might be missing an optimal solution somewhere in that family that you just didn't get to. In my experience, the research agent would almost always lean on the exploitation side. Like, if I, even if I gave it guidance saying, oh, you know, you should explore this family of approaches and keep exploring it until, you know, it doesn't seem like any of the approaches are working and then you can move on to this other thing, it would always just get stuck on that family of approaches. Like, if you just give it open-ended instructions, like, oh, keep exploring until. You don't think there's promise in it, it'll just continue exploring indefinitely. It's very reluctant to fully say, oh, there's no hope in this family of approaches, so I'm going to move on. And I think that is just a byproduct of the fact that this agent is very conservative. It doesn't want to make the claim that, oh, nothing in this family of approaches could work. Until it's tried basically everything you could think of. Most of the times when I was manually intervening to get it back on course, it was forcibly cutting it off, forcing it to like cut off a family and be like, let's move on to something else. And then I'd also specify the family of things to move it on to. So this is an open question. I'm actually not sure how to deal with this. Like I was playing around with just giving an explicit number on, you know, how many experiments you could try, but that's also not optimal, right? Like if one family is a total waste of time, we shouldn't waste any, we should waste barely any experiments on it. But if one family of approaches is promising, we should raise that cap. So, um, I don't know. One thing to consider in the future is having like a dynamic model like that that you know having the model dynamically predict or not predict, judge how many cycles of experiments it should spend on a given family.
- A potential practical consideration is context rot. So I would just leave the researcher agent running in the same window basically all like continuously just so it can benefit from having all the context of all the previous experiments. The only thing though is that sometimes if I would give guidance earlier it seems like after like maybe a day I would come back and you know the model had clearly forgotten some things I'd said the previous day just due to context rot. So, there are a couple of ways we could deal with this. The first is we can just not do anything.
